# Illustrative training avatar

This is an original stylised model inspired by the loose training garments visible in the [Yang Chengfu 1931 plates](https://brennantranslation.wordpress.com/2011/11/24/methods-of-applying-taiji-boxing-taiji-quan-shiyong-fa/) and [Chen Weiming supplement](https://brennantranslation.wordpress.com/2020/06/05/chen-weimings-additional-taiji-photos/). It is not a portrait, body scan, historical clothing reconstruction or DPMesh result. Colours were chosen for this site's palette; the monochrome photographs do not establish them.

The geometry is generated procedurally by `tools/build_avatar.py` in Blender; no scan, photograph-derived mesh, SAM segmentation or DPMesh output is used. Since build v2 the torso, arms and legs are one continuous surface, not an assembly of separate primitives. Simple solids that follow the bones (a lofted torso and tapered limb chains) are fused by a voxel remesh, smoothed, and divided into jacket, trouser, linen-trim and skin zones by material. The head, the hands and the shoes and soles remain separate parts. The body is skinned with Blender's automatic weights, which the script then corrects so that the exposed wrists, ankles and neck follow the joint they cross. Smooth joint bends are a property of this illustrative mesh. They say nothing about how the historical garments moved.

The short jacket, shortened sleeves and raised trouser cuffs are deliberate visibility adaptations. The hands have four separate fingers and a thumb; the feet have distinct shoes and soles. Wrists and ankles remain exposed. The skeleton view retains the head, hands and feet so their orientation can be inspected.

The 24 named bones use a SMPL-style hierarchy, but the geometry and rest positions were authored independently. Matching bone names alone does not establish a correct retargeting: future pose estimates need coordinate, rest-pose, scale and left/right checks.

The two sliders are isolated joint and crouch diagnostics. Neither represents a named historical movement, and catalogue navigation does not change the avatar. No recovered pose or automatic positional binding is present.

Rebuild with `tools/build_avatar.py` in Blender. The builder exports the GLB, an editable blend file, a neutral preview and a machine-generated report. `archive-avatar.glb` is the browser asset. The small model uses 24 bones, 8 mesh objects (one body, the head, two hands, two shoes and two soles) and 16,572 source vertices; glTF may split vertices at primitive boundaries. The armature is unchanged from the first build: the same bone names, hierarchy and rest transforms.
