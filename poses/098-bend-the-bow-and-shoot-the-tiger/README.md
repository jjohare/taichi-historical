# 98. Bend the Bow and Shoot the Tiger

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — Turning stance with separated fists, after Lotus.

## Position

The fists occupy separated lines in a turned stance after the Lotus action.

## Transition

Compare the feet after the kick and before the final advancing punch.

Preceded by: Turn Body and Swing Over Lotus. Followed by: Step Forward, Parry Block and Punch.

## Application study — interpretation

The opposed arm positions suggest paired control and striking possibilities; these remain interpretations.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice the connection across the back without exaggerating the pull of the arms.

## Name and context

The bow title evokes opposing actions; the historical photo is not a literal archery demonstration.
