# 45. Left Strike Tiger

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — Left Strike Tiger, shown from behind.

## Position

The arms form a high and low pair of fists, with the left-oriented stance seen largely from behind.

## Transition

The next Tiger posture changes side; compare the feet to understand the change of direction.

Preceded by: Right Heel Kick. Followed by: Right Strike Tiger.

## Application study — interpretation

The paired levels offer a study of protecting one line while acting on another.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice that both arms belong to one turned trunk.

## Name and context

Tiger names are shared across different postures and do not by themselves prove identical mechanics.
