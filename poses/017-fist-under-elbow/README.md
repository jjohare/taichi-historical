# 17. Fist Under Elbow

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — The low fist sits beneath the opposite elbow.

## Position

A low fist sits beneath the opposite elbow while the forward foot is lightly placed.

## Transition

Compare the narrowing from the previous larger arm pattern before the retreating steps begin.

Preceded by: Embrace the Tiger and Return to Mountain. Followed by: Step Back and Repulse the Monkey, Left.

## Application study — interpretation

The arrangement can suggest paired control and striking lines; the solo image does not establish their exact use.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice the relationship between the upper elbow and lower fist without compressing the chest.

## Name and context

The title describes the conspicuous relationship between the two arms.
