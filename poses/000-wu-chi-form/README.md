# 0. Wu Chi Form

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — Standing preparation; the montage does not separately illustrate the rising and lowering of the arms.

## Position

Upright standing, arms resting at the sides. Read the space between the feet from the source image rather than a modern template.

## Transition

Preparation is the starting reference for Opening; the still does not show the arms rising.

Preceded by: Start of form. Followed by: Opening.

## Application study — interpretation

Study a balanced base from which direction can change. This is an interpretive exercise, not a photographed partner technique.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice contact beneath both feet without making the knees rigid.

## Name and context

Wu Chi is the wording in the supplied list; the historical source presents a preparatory standing posture.
