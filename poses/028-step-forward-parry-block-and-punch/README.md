# 28. Step Forward, Parry Block and Punch

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — This illustration catches a parrying/stepping phase, not the final extended punch; use Chen's sequence to see the completion.

## Position

A fist extends from a stepped stance in the endpoint images. Some montage occurrences instead show the preceding parry.

## Transition

Use the historical sequences to distinguish the turn, step, parry and final extension.

Preceded by: Turn Body and Chop with Fist. Followed by: Step Forward and Grasp the Sparrow's Tail.

## Application study — interpretation

Study clearing a path before the fist travels forward. An isolated punch photograph omits that preparation.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Follow the relationship between the rear foot, trunk and fist rather than reaching from the shoulder alone.

## Name and context

The compound English name covers several actions, so a single plate may not show its named finish.
