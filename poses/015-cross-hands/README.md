# 15. Cross Hands

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — First section boundary.

## Position

The forearms cross in front as the feet come into a more square relationship.

## Transition

This gathering marks a section boundary; the later final occurrence is not separately drawn in the montage.

Preceded by: Apparent Close Up. Followed by: Embrace the Tiger and Return to Mountain.

## Application study — interpretation

Crossing can be explored as receiving and reorganising the arms, without assuming a fixed block.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice the whole stance settling, not only the crossing wrists.

## Name and context

Repeated section boundaries explain why this image appears at several places in the catalogue.
