# 81. Turn Bodyand White Snake Spits out Tongue

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct-with-variant** — The 1931 text distinguishes the open palm in the second phase from the earlier turning fist. Chen's White Snake photographs occur earlier in his sequence; included as a named historical comparison, not proof of identical placement.

## Position

A turning sequence ends with an open hand rather than the earlier fist-led continuation.

## Transition

Compare the two phases and their position late in the sequence; Chen's named comparison occurs earlier.

Preceded by: Fan Through the Back. Followed by: Step Forward, Parry Block and Punch.

## Application study — interpretation

An open-hand continuation after a turn can be explored without claiming identical mechanics across the manuals.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice the change of hand form while keeping the trunk's turn connected.

## Name and context

The snake title is attested in these sources; its placement and execution are not identical across them.
