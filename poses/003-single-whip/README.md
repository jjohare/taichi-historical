# 3. Single whip

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — Hook hand and extended opposite palm identify Single Whip.

## Position

One hand forms a hook while the opposite palm extends, with the feet separated into a broad stance.

## Transition

Track the turn into the open shape; do not treat the hook as an isolated gesture.

Preceded by: Grasp the Sparrow’s  tail. Followed by: Raise Hands and Step Forward.

## Application study — interpretation

The two arms can be studied as separating or controlling lines of contact. The solo plate does not specify a partner's position.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Allow the elbows to retain shape without locking them.

## Name and context

The repeated Single Whips help locate the intervening sequences in the long form.
