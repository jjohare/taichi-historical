# 2. Grasp the Sparrow’s  tail

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — Four montage panels: ward-off, rollback, press and push. Panel 2 is ward-off, not Opening. Some intermediate actions have no montage panel.

## Position

Rounded arms change relationship across ward-off, rollback, press and push. Some occurrences retain only one of these phases.

## Transition

Follow the changes of weight and the turning of the trunk across the image sequence.

Preceded by: Opening. Followed by: Single whip.

## Application study — interpretation

Compare receiving, redirecting and returning pressure as different tasks; these are study interpretations, not a single fixed use.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Keep awareness of the rear foot while looking at the hands.

## Name and context

Bird and sparrow are alternative English wordings in the supplied list. They do not establish different styles.
