# 1. Opening

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**endpoint-reference** — No separate Opening panel exists. Historical standing photographs are endpoint references only, not pictures of the moving opening.

## Position

The archive has standing endpoints but no separate montage image of the moving opening.

## Transition

The change from rest to the first ward-off must be read as movement between pictures, not recovered from one frame.

Preceded by: Wu Chi Form. Followed by: Grasp the Sparrow’s  tail.

## Application study — interpretation

Consider how the arms can lift without lifting the shoulders. A still cannot establish an application by itself.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Let attention widen to the whole body before following a hand.

## Name and context

The absence of an Opening panel is a feature of this montage, not evidence that the action was absent from practice.
