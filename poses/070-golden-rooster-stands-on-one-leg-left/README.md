# 70. Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg, Left

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct-with-naming-caveat** — Standing on the left leg, right knee raised. The 1931 heading names the raised side and calls this right; the supplied list and Chen caption 185 name the standing leg. The 1933 webpage URL ends in photo-186 despite caption 185; caption and actual leg position govern this mapping.

## Position

The left leg supports the body while the right knee and corresponding hand rise.

## Transition

The body gathers upwards from the low posture into a single-leg balance.

Preceded by: Snake Creeps Down. Followed by: Golden Rooster Stands on One Leg, Right.

## Application study — interpretation

The lifted knee and hand suggest coordinated close-range actions; their partner context is not photographed here.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Keep attention on the standing foot and an easy upright balance.

## Name and context

The supplied list names the standing leg. The 1931 source instead names this posture by the raised side.
