# 23. White Crane Spreads its Wings

Historical image study. Modern teaching may differ as the art evolves. Names and numbers are preserved from [moves.md](../../moves.md); notes are editorial observations and interpretations, not a reconstruction of every historical instruction.

## Identification

**direct** — Second White Crane occurrence.

## Position

One hand is high and the other low, with the front foot lightly placed.

## Transition

Compare how the raised hand can descend as the next Brush Knee begins.

Preceded by: Raise Hands and Step Forward. Followed by: Left Brush Knee and Push.

## Application study — interpretation

The contrasting arm heights offer a study of separating upper and lower lines of contact.

## Breathing and attention — editorial suggestions

Breathing and attention are editorial practice suggestions, not recovered instructions for this particular photograph. Allow an easy, unforced breath; do not impose a fixed inhalation or exhalation on each numbered movement.

Notice the length of the upright trunk without raising the shoulders.

## Name and context

The bird name is imagery attached to the posture; this catalogue does not claim an animal-origin story.
