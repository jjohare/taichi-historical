# Photographic sources and reuse

`gibbon-first-five-sets.pdf` is the complete, unmodified 37-page manual by Howard Gibbon, East–West Publications. Downloaded 24 October 2026 from the [publisher’s training site](https://taichionlinetraining.com/wp-content/uploads/Free-Tai-Chi-eBook-Download.pdf).

Page 3 permits electronic distribution without alteration or sale and prohibits printing. This permission applies to the intact PDF, not separately cropped PNGs. No extracted or altered pages are published. Copyright remains with East–West Publications. The text and images are not covered by any repository-wide licence.

Visual inspection of the original PDF pages confirmed the following captions, numbered passages and full-body framing. Original page proportions are retained in the PDF viewer. Inspection does not authenticate the poses as Chee Soo’s historical execution.

Page 30 includes multiple stances under one Crane heading; an isolated image would lose that context. Page 32 labels its middle position Duck; Ewers’s later description uses Monkey. These remain separate teaching records.

## Other visual leads

- [Chee Soo’s original manual, publisher record](https://www.seahorsebooks.co.uk/product/the-chinese-art-of-tai-chi-chuan-by-chee-soo/): publisher says all 140 movements are photographed. Full plates were not available for direct inspection in this research. No redistribution licence or verified per-plate mapping established. Linked, not copied.
- [West Yorkshire Tai Chi video index](https://westyorkshiretaichi.weebly.com/videos.html): 41 video lessons span the complete main form. The final lesson combines the last two named sets. The public publisher index verifies titles and movement ranges; individual frames and spoken instructions have not been audited. Videos load only on request through YouTube’s player, with a direct source fallback. No downloads or thumbnails are rehosted.
- LFIAA illustrated articles: linked alongside relevant descriptions with Keith Ewers named as the later teacher. No reuse licence identified. A later Ewers photo is not labelled as Chee Soo.
- [Publisher biography](https://seahorsebooks.co.uk/chee-soo-biography/): its archer-like photograph is captioned as the **Tai Chi dance**. Excluded from main-form pose matching, even though the main form also has archer names.
- Angie Wood’s course pages include decorative imagery and generic course images. These have not been used as pose evidence.

No Lee position has been bound to a 3D model or a Yang photograph.
